Combining screenshots works best when you decide what each image contributes before adjusting the controls. This guide covers the multi-image process for two to sixteen images; with one photo, the canvas stays single and you can add a signature without choosing a layout. You can also choose a layout first to see its empty slots, then fill them with photos.

## 1. Decide the viewing order

1. Use the first image for the most important view, such as a full-body shot or the main pose.
2. Put the back view, alternate pose, or close-up after the main image.
3. Keep shots with similar lighting and framing together so the final canvas feels intentional.

## 2. Upload and reorder the cards

1. Open **Image source** on desktop or **Photo** on mobile.
2. Click the empty image cards and upload the files in the order you want to see them.
3. Use **Swap Order** to exchange the first two cards.
4. Use the left or right movement control on a card when you only need to move it one position.
5. Use **Change** when you want to replace one screenshot without rebuilding the composition.

## 3. Pick the layout for the number of images

1. Open **Layout** on desktop or the mobile **Layout** sheet.
2. With two images, choose **Split** for a side-by-side comparison.
3. Choose **Vertical** when the top-to-bottom order matters, such as a full-body shot followed by a detail shot.
4. With three or four images, choose **Grid** to create its four-slot 2×2 board. With three images, one slot remains empty. For exactly 9 or 16 images, choose the **3×3** or **4×4 grid** template.

## 4. Choose a canvas ratio

1. The default **Original ratio** keeps the natural composition intact.
2. Choose the **X timeline** profile for a 16:9 wide result that reads clearly in the timeline. Check the downloaded file because the destination platform may crop unusual ratios.
3. Choose **2:1** when the images should form a long horizontal strip.
4. On mobile, change this from **Export → Ratio Settings**. The export ratio and the on-screen preview zoom are separate controls.

## 5. Tune the gaps and seams

1. Choose **None** under **Transition** when you want a clear gap between frames.
2. Adjust **Image Gap** from 0 to 100px until the spacing is even.
3. Choose **Soft Blend** when you want neighboring images to overlap.
4. Adjust **Blend Width** gradually. A very wide blend can cover important details near the edge of a frame.
5. Add **Border Width** when the composition needs a consistent outer margin.

## 6. Correct each crop

1. Select one image card at a time.
2. Use the **Selected image** scale slider or enter a value between 50% and 300%.
3. Drag the selected image on the canvas so faces, weapons, and outfit details are visible.
4. Scroll over the selected image for quick zoom adjustments.
5. Use **Reset** if one image becomes difficult to frame, then adjust it again from the default position.
6. Turn on **Lock Image Position** after all cards are aligned.

## 7. Add the finishing layers

1. Choose a background under **Surface finish**: **White**, **Black**, **Light Gray**, **Transparent**, or **Custom**.
2. Keep **Film Grain** at 0% unless a small amount of texture helps unify the screenshots.
3. If needed, open **Signature → Text** and add a character name and server label.
4. If you have a Free Company mark, add it from **Signature → Logo Upload** and keep it away from the main subject.

## 8. Save and review

1. Check that the first card is still the visual lead and that no face or important detail is clipped.
2. Click **Export PNG** on desktop or use **Export → Save Photo** on mobile. PNG is preferred; if an opaque result exceeds 5 MB, the editor saves a high-quality JPEG instead.
3. Open the downloaded file once before posting. If an edge is clipped, return to the active image card and correct its scale or position.
